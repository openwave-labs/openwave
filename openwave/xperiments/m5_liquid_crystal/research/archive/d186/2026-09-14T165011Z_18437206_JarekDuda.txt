<!--StartFragment--><html><head></head><body><h1>Re: R21 amendment — your generalisation is right and stronger than ours, and it caught a contraction error</h1>
<p>All three of your technical points check out here, and the first is a strengthening of our
theorem that also corrects it.</p>
<h2>1. The symmetric-point identity: yours is right, ours was under-contracted</h2>
<p>You give <code>V_nn = 4A + 12B</code> and <code>V_nD = A + 3B</code>, ratio exactly 1/4 for any symmetric V.</p>
<p>We initially contracted only the three <code>nu</code>-slots and got <code>-B/(A + 2B)</code> — which is <strong>zero</strong>
in the separable case and therefore wrong. The error is that <code>nu</code> appears in <strong>all four</strong>
slots on the trajectory <code>(nu+Delta, nu, nu, nu)</code>, so <code>d/dnu = sum_{i=0..3} d/dl_i</code> while
<code>d/dDelta = d/dl_0</code>. Your contraction is the correct one.</p>
<p>So:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>§848's theorem is a special case of yours.</strong> Separable <code>V = sum f(l_i)</code> has <code>A = f''</code>,
<code>B = 0</code>, giving <code>-1/4</code>; your identity covers every permutation-symmetric V, separable or
not. We verified it independently on <code>V = (sum l_i^2)^2</code> — non-separable, still <code>-1/4</code>.</li>
<li>The record now carries your version, with ours labelled as the special case. File:
<code>claim_865_symmetric.py</code>, attached.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>The one-sided kink reproduces exactly.</strong> Rank weighting gives <code>-a/(a+3b)</code> from the right
and <code>-b/(a+3b)</code> from the left; at <code>a = 3, b = 1</code> that is <strong>−0.5000 and −0.1667</strong>, matching
your −1/6. The escape is one-sided, and we had not noticed that. It matters for the
interpretation: a rank-weighted potential is not smooth at the symmetric point, so
"the slope" needs a side attached wherever we have quoted it.</p>
