<p><strong>2. κ_s is now pinned down to a fork</strong> (Sol, replayed independently).</p>
<ul>
<li>An induced hadronic value is 0.009, far too small.</li>
<li>Standard-Model-size matter over a Planck window gives 2.0, and the measured weak coupling gives 2.35.</li>
<li>So the proton is protected only if the spectator curvature has weak strength, with B − L and B mod 3 as the selection rule (ΔB = ΔL = 3).</li>
<li>Hence the fork: independent gauge links on the coframe's left index, or induced couplings, which need charged matter beyond the Standard Model.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>4. Light, after today's discussion.</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Why 2108's Maxwell derivation doesn't give radiation yet.</strong>
<ul>
<li>The Euler–Lagrange equations for A_μ = [M, ∂_μM] give Maxwell exactly if A is varied freely (2108, Sec. IV.A).</li>
<li>But A is built from M. For any plane wave M(k·x), A_μ = k_μ[M, M′] is a pure gradient, so F = 0.</li>
<li>The proper M-variation (Eq. 38) has no linear part around the uniform vacuum.</li>
<li>Statics and Coulomb are unaffected. Radiation needs a field linear in the rotations.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>A candidate, in liquid-crystal terms.</strong>
<ul>
<li><strong>The term:</strong> a quadratic elastic term with zero splay and equal twist and bend (K₁ = 0, K₂ = K₃), i.e. K|∇×n|². This is MacCullagh's rotational aether, which is Maxwell's equations in temporal gauge.</li>
<li><strong>What it fixes:</strong> the radial hedgehog is pure splay (∇×r̂ = 0), so this term costs it nothing. That removes the 16πL₁-per-box cost we found on 18 Nov.</li>
<li><strong>What it gives:</strong> transverse tilts propagate; longitudinal ones have no restoring force.</li>
<li><strong>What it needs:</strong>
<ul>
<li>three tilt components, so that every direction has two polarizations, e.g. the biaxial frame or the time axis's three tilts;</li>
<li>a covariant form, F = du for the time axis, which is the photon as the Nambu–Goldstone mode of the time axis;</li>
<li>one Maxwell field: the curvature's Coulomb field and the curl term's radiation must join.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If it is linear, Coulomb (2108, Fig. 2) and Lorentz-invariant, Feynman's argument then gives Maxwell.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>The quantum side.</strong>
<ul>
<li><strong>Single photons:</strong> a linear wake plus an indivisible unit carrying ħω and ħ, guided by it, accounts for them locally (Grangier's anticorrelation, delayed choice, average photon trajectories).</li>
<li><strong>Correlations:</strong> these come from one pin per registration, forced by Born's rule at a constant rate (BellSolitons v6.0, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23860500). The two-atom cascade's selection rule is such a pin, and the first photon's colour then predicts the second's.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
