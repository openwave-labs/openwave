## 3. A plan for your third discriminator (M5.56)

Your question: whether, on any of OpenWave's actions, a swap of two cores forces a relative $2\pi$ turn of the frame, or the frame can relax through contact. The plan: [`m5_56_task_details.md`](https://github.com/openwave-labs/openwave/blob/main/openwave/xperiments/m5_liquid_crystal/research/tasks/m5_56_task_details.md). We read your release v4.5 for it, pages 234, 245 to 246 and 346 to 348. In short:

**The action and the object.** The action of your post of 2026-11-20 with the midpoint weight ($L_V = 1$, $L_1 = 0$, $\delta = 0$), as ported in M5.51, and its ring. The vacuum is uniaxial, so the frame we read is the long axis.

**The turn.** Every term of the action is unchanged by one constant rotation $Q$ of the matrix with the positions kept, $M \mapsto Q M Q^{\mathsf T}$, on the lattice exactly. The instrument turns the field by an angle that depends on the distance $\rho$ from the ring's center:

$$
M(x) \mapsto Q(x) M(x) Q(x)^{\mathsf T} , \qquad Q(x) = \exp\big( \Theta f(\rho) [a]_{\times} \big)
$$

with $f = 1$ inside a radius $R_1$ beyond the ring and falling linearly to 0 at $R_2$ inside the faces. For $\Theta = 2\pi$ the ring and the faces are untouched exactly, with no resampling, and only the collar holds a texture: the $2\pi$ turn of the target alone, for which your release gives a collar invariant of 1 on your relaxed ring (p. 246).

**What is already known, from fixed fields (no relaxation).** The plan's first draft would have relaxed that turn with the ring free and asked whether it stays. Its audit showed that a way down exists before any run. Lowering $\Theta$ from $2\pi$ to 0 turns the target on the whole ball inside $R_1$, the ring included, by one rotation, which costs nothing inside the ball: no term of the action pairs a matrix index with a spatial one. The elastic term $L_1 \lVert \partial M \rVert^2$ of the earlier actions is unchanged by it as well, so this does not rest on $L_1 = 0$. Along that path, on our stored state of half-box 12 at spacing 0.5 ($R_1 = 4.354$, $R_2 = 11$, the turn about the ring's normal), the energy above the unturned state reads 668.0, 347.5, 171.4, 81.3, 37.9, 17.2, 6.9, 1.7 and 0 at $\Theta/\pi = 2, 1.75, \dots, 0$: a fall at every step, with every node's eigenvalues kept. So a free core can shed a full turn of the target with no tear, no barrier and no contact; which route a descent takes is a separate read. On the seed of the pair the same kind of path also falls at every step. Most of that energy is lattice cost (a like turn on the hedgehog seed costs 611, 397 and 235 at spacings 0.5, 0.375 and 0.25); the monotone fall is the statement, not the number.

**So the plan has three rungs.**

1. **The free core.** The path above, tabled by tracked code on three grids, and on the pair if two rings stand, and a few chains that read which route a descent takes, two of them with two optimizers. A release there is stated in advance as showing nothing held and nothing about contact.
2. **The held core, the main rung.** The same $2\pi$ turn with the ball inside $R_1$ held as the faces are: the setting in which a turn can be kept. Relaxed on half-box 12 at spacings 0.5 and 0.375 and on half-box 15, two axes, two collar widths: the turn is HELD (still there at a converged end, with no tear read; printed with its energy against the box and the spacing), or TORN (a node outside the held ball where the gap between the top two eigenvalues falls under 0.5, or a jump between neighbors), or released with no tear read, which would be reported as unexplained. A turn of $4\pi$ is run beside it as a probe of whether the descent unwinds the trivial class.
3. **The pair.** Two rings from a seed of degree 2 in a box of half-width 18. If two rings stand, both balls are held and three starts are relaxed: a full turn of one ring against the other; the swap, built as a half-turn of the region that holds both; and a full internal turn around the pair, which is what that half-turn leaves in the far zone (for a far field of weight 2 a spatial turn by $\alpha$ is an internal turn by $-2\alpha$). The swap is run only if the relaxed pair is symmetric under the half-turn and the built field holds no jump between neighbors. Read: whether each start is held, at what energy, and whether the swap and the full turn of one ring end alike.

**The sign of a turn** is read by following the rotation of each spherical shell against the same shell of the unturned state, from the faces inward, as a unit quaternion.

**What it cannot say.** It is a descent of a static field, not a motion of the cores: it reads no exchange sign, no spin and no statistics. The held balls are a constraint of the plan, not a state of the action. It is $\delta = 0$ only, in closed boxes. A lattice can tear where a continuum cannot, which the second spacing is there to show. The two-ring state may not exist. The energy of the path your release marks as not computed, the order-two swap through a ring's hole (p. 246), is not computed by this plan, nor is a merger of two cores.
