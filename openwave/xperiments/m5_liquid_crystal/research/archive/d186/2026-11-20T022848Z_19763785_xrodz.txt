## 1. Charge quantization: M5.46

The relaxed unit of the M5.42 action (κ = v = 1, δ = 0.05, L₁ = 0.03) was pulled by a dilation to separations 10 and 13.8 and released with free faces, at spacings 0.5 and 0.25, the charge read on nested surfaces by an instrument that tracks the branch. With at least 3.1 of room to the faces it relaxed back as one loop; every liftable surface around it read 1; nothing read ½. In the tight box (13.8 in half-box 8) the faces cut the loop into arcs. In a box it could leave, the continued states left as one set (on chains that carry no label). This is the outcome of your point 2.

Two limits keep the cell at partial, and the record states both: a whole value holds by construction on a liftable surface, the value 1 being inherited from the seed; and a release reads the sign of the force at two separations without holding one, so a split is not excluded. The cell's way back is now: a ladder in which the separation of the two +½ lines is held and raised in steps, the energy still rising where the box ends and no surface reading ½, also under a perturbation that seeks a split.

Record on OpenWave: [`m5_46_task_details.md`](https://github.com/openwave-labs/openwave/blob/main/openwave/xperiments/m5_liquid_crystal/research/tasks/m5_46_task_details.md); the ladder, [`m5_50_task_details.md`](https://github.com/openwave-labs/openwave/blob/main/openwave/xperiments/m5_liquid_crystal/research/tasks/m5_50_task_details.md).

## 2. Klein-Gordon: M5.47

**The time part.** The M5.42 module codes the inertia on the twist only, so the plan supplied a time part: the form whose value on the twist is that inertia,

$$
T = h^3 \sum_n \Big[ L_1 \lVert \dot M \rVert^2 + \kappa \sum_i \lVert [\dot M, \partial_i M] \rVert^2 \Big]
$$

evolved with a fourth-order Runge-Kutta scheme on the field and its momentum, the outer shell held. Every statement below is conditional on that reading. Its kinetic density in the mid polarization is L₁ q(x), with q a dimensionless kinetic factor equal to 1 in the vacuum; that density is the I(r) of your post, so your local mass $\sqrt{L_1 m^2 / I}$ is our $\sqrt{m^2_{\mathrm{vac}} / q}$.

**The vacuum.** Six branches follow $\omega^2 = \hat k^2 + m^2$ within 0.16 %, on twenty sine modes at two spacings, with $m^2$ = 0.1057, 60.089 and 473.46 and three massless rotation branches; with v doubled the three masses double. Yours are 0.10575, 60.0887 and 473.470. This follows from the declared action, whose Hessian gives 0.10574, 60.0891 and 473.474.

**The relaxed unit.** Small noise in the mid polarization was evolved on four grids (half-boxes 8 and 10, spacings 0.5 and 0.25), and the local wavenumber read from Fourier sums at seventeen frequencies from 3 to 8, in bins of q. The kinetic factor runs from 1.4 to 6.6 over the region read, with a tail of 1 + 88/r², so no part of these boxes is vacuum; by that tail, which is an extrapolation, q − 1 falls under 0.05 only beyond r near 42.

- **The readings frozen before the run read NOT HELD** at spacing 0.25 on both boxes: one law with a single mass left free, a local law per bin, and the linearized action in one polarization; so does the comparison of the two boxes, and the comparison of the two spacings carries no label. One cause is visible: the frozen fits joined two kinds of waves, since under a bin's cutoff more than 90 % of the response is outside the mid polarization, in the directions that rotate the frame or split the two small eigenvalues. It is not the only cause.
- **Added during the run, and carrying no label:** above its cutoff each bin lies near a line of its own. At spacing 0.25 the slope $c^2$ is 0.86 to 0.91 (0.83 to 0.95 at spacing 0.5), and the mass $m^2$ falls as q rises: from 34.5 to 9.4 on the larger box and from 29.7 to 8.9 on the smaller, against 60.09 in the vacuum. The cutoff of each bin sits 2 to 8 % under $\sqrt{m^2_{\mathrm{vac}} / q}$; the run names no cause for that gap. The two boxes agree within 0.013 in $c^2$ and 5.7 % in $m^2$; the two spacings differ by up to 0.073 in $c^2$.
- **The limits of that descriptor.** It was defined after three of the four grids had been read; on the fourth, three bins of ten sit more than 5 % from their fit (up to 8.6 %). Above the cutoff the mid wave still shares its power with the other polarizations (20 to 78 % outside it at spacing 0.25), and the descriptor assumes those waves weigh little in the read, which is not tested. The two speeds of the linearized action (along the gradient of the frame and across it) are not resolved, and the estimator has no known-answer control below a squared wavenumber of about 4, where each cutoff is read.

The controls took three tries (the step, the noise amplitudes and the length changed; no threshold changed), and two independent audits read the record.

**Your registered bands.** This run drove nothing and read nothing along a ray, so it does not test your bands. They are filed as M5.49: the band of each ray is computed from the OpenWave background before the wave is looked at, then read. M5.47 holds Fourier sums at ω = 4 and 5.5 on four grids; whether those are enough, or a driven run is needed, is decided when M5.49 is staged. With M5.49 the cell's way back also asks for one mass, in a box wide enough to reach it. One note on your post: the link of the archive is empty there (the upload did not complete).

## 3. Your predictions P1 to P3: M5.48

| Prediction | Reads | Deciding numbers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | HELD, start-dependent, by the rules of our plan | at J = J_e/320: the excess falls by 0.377 and ω by 0.375 without L₁ (your threshold 0.20); with L₁ = 0.03 they change by 0.0015 and 0.0013 (your threshold 0.02) |
| P2 | NOT HELD, on the continued chain | converged at I·h = 993.09 (I = 5296.50): 0.69 % under the 1000 of the window, and 3.5 under the 5300 of its other form; U = 14.6924, inside its window |

**P1 in more words.** "A fixed J small enough that the J = 0 structure survives" needed a rule. Ours: six candidates from J_e/40 to J_e/1280, a survival test in five clauses, one J for both arms, and the largest J at which the four states from the seed pass. That gives J_e/320. Four things should be read beside the word:

- At J_e/320 the states without L₁ barely respond (their inertia rises by 1.3 and 0.7 %), so the falls restate the J = 0 arithmetic of M5.45, 1 − I(0.375)/I(0.25) = 0.3785.
- The word rests on two of our clauses at J_e/40. By the reads of the pool, J_e/40 was excluded by one clause on one state: the loop radius of the state without L₁ at spacing 0.25 changes by 16.1 % against the 10 % the clause allows, a change about half the spread of the reader's own radii. A return chain added after the audit, with the word in view and carrying no word, showed that with that clause read as a loop count alone J_e/40 still fails, because the return does not close in U. It survives only if the return clause is also read one-sided.
- At the larger candidates the half without L₁ still holds (falls of 0.34 to 0.51), but with L₁ = 0.03 the changes are 0.18 to 0.67, on states that, at the two largest candidates, put 28 to 81 % of their inertia on a single node. So wherever J_e/40 is the J, with one J per arm or under the looser reading above, the half with L₁ reads not held. Our survival clauses did not exclude those one-node states; a clause on the smoothness of the inertia would, and then the word is held under every variant we tried.
- One of the twelve pairs of states without L₁ reads 0.193 in both falls. Your falsifier (a change of the excess under 0.05) is met at none of the four decided candidates.

**P2.** The chain of M5.45 at spacing 0.1875 was continued by the same rule and converged after 7173 more iterations; the inertia moved by less than 0.001 % from where it had been cut. It is one start; nothing is said about other states of that grid.
