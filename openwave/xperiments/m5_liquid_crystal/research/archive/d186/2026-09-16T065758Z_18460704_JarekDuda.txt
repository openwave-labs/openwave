## I. Retractions

These were all things I believed and in three cases had written down as results.

**The redshift does not select the Q-ball.** I claimed a free-phase rotor redshifts as
`e^{-4U}` and is therefore excluded. False, and no relaxation argument is needed. For a rotor
of fixed *proper* inertia in a static lapse,
`S = ∫dτ (I_p/2)(dθ/dτ)² = ∫dt (I_p/2N)θ̇²`, so `J = I_pθ̇/N` and `θ̇ = NJ/I_p ∝ √(−g₀₀)`.
Even a rigid rotor redshifts correctly. My `e^{-4U}` held the *coordinate* inertia fixed while
varying the metric — it changed the apparatus rather than moving it. The note's title changed
accordingly.

**The `Q₈` no-go for Fermi statistics.** I argued that a first-order term is a flat `U(1)`
connection, hence a one-dimensional character of `π₁`, hence trivial on the centre of `Q₈`,
hence incapable of giving the soliton a `−1`. The premise is false: `B[M]θ̇` is flat only if `B`
is constant along the trajectory, and a genuine Wess–Zumino term has curvature — the
spin-coherent connection `S(1−cosθ)dφ` has `F = S sinθ dθ∧dφ ≠ 0`. Worse, the counterexample
is the one I had cited two paragraphs earlier as the precedent. Route (i) is open.

**A twelve-order clock–Coulomb collision.** I derived `ω = Λ_t/|ζ|` on a tilted shell, fixing
`Λ_t ~ 10⁵ eV` against Coulomb's `< 3×10⁻⁸ eV`. It omitted the inertia the tilt stiffness
itself contributes: being a two-derivative term with no spatial derivative in its time part, its
inertia grows as `R³` and dominates. Redone, `ω` acquires a `J`-dependence and the number does
not follow.

**A superluminal trap for kinetic-term clocks.** I wrote `c_s² = 1 + 2uK′/K` and concluded that
binding and causality were incompatible. That is not the k-essence sound speed and not the sound
speed of anything: for `P` linear in the kinetic invariant, `P_XX = 0` and `c_s² = 1` exactly,
for any `K`.

**An orbifold obstruction at the clock core.** I claimed the eigenvalue plane is `ℝ²/ℤ₃`, a cone
of deficit `4π/3` whose apex is where every melted core sits. It quotients the wrong space: the
physical field is `Q`, the traceless symmetric `3×3` matrices are `ℝ⁵`, smooth at `Q = 0`. A
line through the origin is smooth in `Q` while `r = |t|` kinks — a coordinate singularity.

Two of these came from tests with silent bookkeeping faults (an ascending eigenvalue sort that
selected a transverse projector instead of the time axis; a six-element basis for a
five-dimensional space), both producing cleaner results than the physics supported. The note
carries a status table listing every principal claim as exact, conditional, or withdrawn.

## II. What survived, exactly

**The quartic *is* Faber's dual-Maxwell energy.** On the degenerate-pair vacuum,
`F_ij = −(1−δ)²ρ_ij[n]_×` (verified on 500 random jets; the overall sign is a convention, the
energy is not), giving `Σ⟨F_ij,F_ij⟩ = 4(1−δ)⁴|E_top|²` exactly. Hence
**`e² = 16πc₄(1−δ)⁴`**, so with `c₄ = 1` and Gaussian units `1−δ = 0.110`. And by Helmholtz plus
Cauchy–Schwarz the **charge-even channel is exactly zero**: the record's "Newton sign" cell
should read *absent*, not *repels*. One caveat added since the referee note: the cross-term
cancellation uses a boundary term at the excised cores that we have checked only indirectly.

**The one defect.** Rotation×rotation and boost×boost are both positive; only rotation×boost is
negative. The negative block couples **charge and clock to mass, never mass to mass**, so the
original sign-flip mechanism could not have worked even with the ghost.

**Scale-mode gravity, with one honest condition.** `Q = E/2` for static solitons by Derrick.
For a *rotating* soliton there are two inertias — quadratic (`C₂ ~ λ³`) and quartic
(`C₄ ~ λ`) — and `2Q − E` vanishes identically in the coefficients **iff** the `χ`-weights are
`(p₂,p₄) = (8,4)`, which minimal coupling supplies as `p(n_s) = 8 − 2n_s` for every term. That
is a consistency statement about a self-generated metric rather than a derivation, and it holds
in one of two weight assignments the note states. Also: `γ = β = 1`, `η_N = 0`, and
`Ġ/G = 0` — the last only with the covariant kinetic term `κ[e^{8χ}χ̇² − |∇χ|²]`, since the
original form put the scalar and matter cones a factor `e^{4χ}` apart.

**One near-term prediction: `0.73 μas`.** Second-order solar deflection, `11.65` against GR's
`10.92`. It depends on `b₂`, not on the `U³` coefficient as I first said — and every reciprocal
metric (`AB = 1`) with `β = γ = 1` has `b₂ = 2`, so this is fixed for the class rather than
tunable. Note Gaia's 45° solar-aspect scanning law means it never observes near the limb.

## III. Two things the stacks can falsify

**1. Relaxed like and unlike pairs on the degenerate-pair vacuum.** Predicted
`U(++) → +32π(1−δ)⁴/d` from above with lattice residual `∝ h/d²`, and
`U(+−) = −32π(1−δ)⁴/d` to lattice precision. If it holds we have the Coulomb certificate with
the charge in closed form. Two ansatz traps: the collinear rational-map product has the wrong
local degree when both centres sit on the stereographic axis, and the quotient map creates a
director-flip tube costing `∝ L/d²`.

**2. `C₁₂` versus the biaxiality gap on *relaxed* clocks.** The shipped `cross_inertia.py`
scans seven gaps and crosses between 0.18 and 0.14 (the 0.22/0.16 numbers I quoted earlier came
from a coarser separate run — @giuliano-vantasner was right to flag it). The crossing sits where
the degenerate exterior that localises the clock begins. If a localised clock exists on the
attractive side, two problems close at once.

## IV. The open problem, and the current best candidate

**The free-inertia escape is open on the corrected Lagrangian.** A tilted *uniaxial* shell
carries clock inertia at zero **static** cost — the quartic vanishes (one jet), `κ₀₀(∂ln λ₀)²`
vanishes (constant eigenvalues), `κ₂₃w(D)` vanishes identically at `λ₂ = λ₃`, and `V` is
spectrum-preserving. With `β = af(r/R)` and `a² ∝ R⁻²` both the energy and the frequency vanish
as `1/R` at fixed charge, so **no positive stiffness gaps a massless mode carrying the clock
charge**, whatever its coefficient.

Two ways out have been found, both outside the classes we had tested.

**A splay-free elastic term.** The Coulomb hedgehog is *pure splay* — twist `7.7×10⁻²⁹`, bend
`0` — while the tilted shell carries bend `0.68`. So tail-blindness and escape-charging are not
the same condition, and a twist-bend combination could charge one and not the other. It lives in
the `L₂,L₃` invariants that have never been enumerated for `SO(1,3)`. Caveat: its energy is also
linear in `R`, so the same `a² ∝ R⁻²` choice defeats it unless no profile family does.

**A spectral clock charge — the strongest candidate.** Put the clock `U(1)` on two spectral
amplitudes of the *proper* spatial matrix rather than on director rotation: with `A = log B`,
`b = ⅓tr A`, `q = √(tr(A−b)²)`, `S = (A−b)/q`,
`Φ = (F/√2)[b − b_v + i log(q/q_v)]`. Director Goldstones carry **none** of this charge, so the
halo cannot absorb it and the escape closes by construction. Three things we checked: `b` is
exactly scale-invariant on the *proper* matrix (on the raw one it would shift by `−log s` and
the clock would rotate the gravitational mode); the non-isotropic patch is forced, since at an
isotropic `B` the tangent representation is `1⊕5` with no non-trivial commuting compact `U(1)`;
and the core stays regular, `min‖Z‖ = 0.263`, because `S_∞` is uniaxial and `S_c` maximally
biaxial — different orbits, never proportional.

Its cost is that it **inverts the core**, from melted-and-isotropic to maximally biaxial. The
FLS lightening, the `q⁴` mechanism and the K-ball were all solving the old problem and do not
carry over. And `μ²(S) = m_c² + (m_v²−m_c²)·6[tr S³]²` is minimised on a whole `SO(3)` orbit, so
the core carries a two-parameter orientational degeneracy — extra zero modes any stability
analysis has to include.

**The next calculation is small and decides a lot.** `Φ` is built from two scale-invariant
scalars, so the clock sector's fields carry `χ`-weight zero and the sector takes its weight
entirely from the metric factors. That is directly testable against `p(n_s) = 8 − 2n_s`. Since
the whole gravity result rests on that identity, it should be run before the coupled defect.

## V. Scoreboard states

I'd suggest three, kept distinct, because collapsing them is how most of my retractions
happened:

- **excluded within this class** — naming the class every time;
- **reduced model solved** — flat space, one sector, numerically verified;
- **full coupled model untested**.

Every closure in the note is of the first kind. Claim 13 is about effective metrics built from
one scalar; the Fierz–Pauli result is about two-derivative terms on `M`'s orientation; route (b)
is about the projector-diagonal `κ₁₂,κ₁₃`. None is a theorem about all Lagrangians, and the two
mechanisms above were both found outside the tested families.

Happy to be shown wrong on any of it — that has been the productive direction so far.
