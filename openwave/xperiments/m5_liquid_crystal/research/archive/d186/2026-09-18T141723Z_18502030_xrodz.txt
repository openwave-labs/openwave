# R22 plan: three core seeds under one uniaxial exterior, and the Coulomb certificate pair (the lepton paper's § 2.1 protocol and the 09-15 pair gate, on the 4x4 field with the polish as the instrument)

## TL;DR

1. R22 moves to the exactly uniaxial vacuum `(g, 1, delta, delta)` that the working note (Zenodo 22788604, § 4 (iv)) and the lepton paper (Zenodo 22801032, § 2.1) both use. The R20 and R21 rows on `(g, 1, delta, 0)` stay in the record as the biaxial control.
2. R22-1 runs the lepton paper's § 2.1 protocol on the full 4x4 field: several inequivalent one-charge core seeds, one common exterior, one action, polished to a stated residual, under a pinned exterior and a released one, at two resolutions and two boxes. The question is whether any branch separation belongs to the field.
3. R22-2 runs the pair gate of the 09-15 posts: like and unlike pairs with pinned cores on the degenerate-pair vacuum, against `U = -/+ 32 pi (1 - delta)^4 / d` and the charge-even half-sum going to zero.
4. R22-0 is the form level, run first: the Faber identity on our stencils and its extension to the biaxial vacuum, the `epsilon^4` flatness of `V4` on the degenerate pair, the posted scripts run unchanged, one line per claim.
5. One question for the author at the end, and a section for Maciej on the L-ladder.

## What R21 already says about the three-lepton question

The lepton paper reviews our stack at an August snapshot, so it predates R20 and R21. Its § 2.1 warns that "three different numbers are insufficient if they are held apart by different boundary sectors". R21 measured exactly that on the three-axis hedgehogs of the biaxial vacuum ([the R21 results post](https://github.com/vantasnerdan/substrate-framework/discussions/186#discussioncomment-18441370)):

| Axis | Seed pin | Relaxed far pin | Free boundary | Winding on the r 9 surface |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `S_1` | 5.68 | 5.27 | 4.17 | kept (the audit's count is 1 on every boundary; 4 to 6 conflicting links under two of them) |
| `S_d` | 55.13 | 6.16 | 1.27 | non-orientable on every boundary (a disclination texture) |
| `S_0` | 15.52 | 3.95 | 1.73 | lost on every boundary |

The three energies were the pin's, and two of the three objects were not one-charge hedgehogs once polished. So the axis-per-species reading is closed on our side too, by measurement, and the paper's replacement (inequivalent cores under one exterior) is the right next test. One R21 row carries over: at `W1 x 25` the electron is compact and boundary-indifferent (13.00 pinned, 12.68 free, 2.4 percent apart) with the spectrum held, so `W1 x 25` is the working potential scale and `W1 x 1` the control.

## One question for the author

Both new documents fix the vacuum as exactly uniaxial, so R22 takes `(g, 1, delta, delta)` as current. What is not fixed is the potential on it: `V4` is `epsilon^4`-flat there (note § 8.6), and the sextic Landau-de Gennes family of eq. (8) has no declared coefficients. R22 runs the certified `V4`. If there is a preferred member of eq. (8), or a preferred delta between 0.3 and the 0.89 of the `c4 = 1` fit, one line with the values is enough and the same seeds rerun on it.
