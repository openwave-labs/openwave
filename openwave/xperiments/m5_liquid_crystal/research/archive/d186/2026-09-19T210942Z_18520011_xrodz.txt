## The stiffness term (R23-0)

With `N = M eta` and the vacuum spectrum `(-g, 1, delta, delta)`, a potential `V(tr N, ..., tr N^4)` keeps that spectrum critical only if its first derivatives `V_p` satisfy `sum_p p V_p x^(p-1) = 0` at the three distinct eigenvalues. A cubic with three fixed roots is fixed up to a factor, so the first derivatives have one free direction:

```text
L = sum_p a_p (tr N^p - C_p),     sum_p p a_p x^(p-1) = (x + g)(x - 1)(x - delta)
V = V4 + c L
split delta ± eps :   V = (g + delta)(1 - delta) abs(c) eps^2 + O(eps^4)     for c < 0
```

For `c > 0` the vacuum is unstable along the split. At g 8, delta 0.3 the coefficient is `5.81 abs(c)`. A finite-difference Hessian at `W1 x 25` and `c = -1e-4` shows 5 zero modes, the doublet at `0.0321 w` twice, and the three massive modes moved by under 1 percent. Below, `c` means `abs(c)`.

R23-0 checks, before any lattice row: the uniqueness statement; the vacuum as the global minimum of `V4 + c L` over the scan's `c` range (random spectra); the closed-form doublet eigenvalue; the per-cell `M_00` solve of the R22-1s instrument ([`m5_32_r22_1s_slaved.py`](https://github.com/openwave-labs/openwave/blob/6f4e952dc9b0fc37d367f321482e486a2695377c/openwave/xperiments/m5_liquid_crystal/research/scripts/m5_32_r22_1s_slaved.py)) extended to `V4 + c L`, with its reduced gradient against a complex-step derivative and with `c = 0` returning the R22-1s first-chunk energy to 1e-10; the conversion of `M^2 / V_0` into `c` for each candidate definition of `V_0`.

## The two exponents

The reply's picture is a Klein-Gordon field with a constant stiffness `kappa`, so the halo is cut at `1 / sqrt(c / kappa)` and the radius goes as `c^(-1/2)`.

On our stack the split has no gradient term of its own. Its stiffness is induced by the hedgehog through the quartic, and the R22-1 audit measured it on the exact uniaxial exterior ([`m5_32_r22_1_audit.py`](https://github.com/openwave-labs/openwave/blob/6f4e952dc9b0fc37d367f321482e486a2695377c/openwave/xperiments/m5_liquid_crystal/research/scripts/m5_32_r22_1_audit.py), check Q5, reproduced to 0.3 percent on clean shells):

```text
e_2 = (K / r^2) [ 2 eps_r^2 + 2 eps^2 / r^2 ],     K = 8 (1 - delta)^2
with m2 eps^2 added, m2 = (g + delta)(1 - delta) c :
eps'' = eps / r^2 + (m2 / 2K) r^2 eps
```

Without the potential the decaying solution is `r^(-0.618)`; the audit read log slopes of -0.4 to -0.9 on the R22 end fields, which is why they fill the box. With the potential the tail is cut as a Gaussian at `R ~ (2K / m2)^(1/4)`, up to angular factors of order one, so the radius goes as `c^(-1/4)`. On this picture the scan range below spans `R` from about 4.6 to about 14.6 in a half-box of 24.

Both are hypotheses. The second one comes from an audited second variation but its cutoff has not been run.
