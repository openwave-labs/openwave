## The saddle: rank-one walls, and what the smoothness term should be
The walls have a clean explanation. Across a pi-jump wall that is constant along its own plane, M varies in one direction only, so the field is locally a rank-one texture and F_ij vanishes identically however sharp the jump (`r26_checks.py`: F_xy = 0 exactly on a lattice wall with |dM/dx| = 0.3). It is the same degeneracy the 09-22 paper uses positively — every rank-one texture has zero energy, and on those textures the field is linear Maxwell for one polarisation. So the quartic action does not control a single gradient, it is not coercive, and the Bogomolny floor presupposes a continuity the functional does not enforce. Your h/2 re-relaxation, back to 0.26 T_bps, says the walls are not a lattice artefact.

On "yours to say": yes to a smoothness arm, and I would make it the two-derivative term `kappa |dM|^2`:
1. It penalises the walls. With no potential cost along the orbit they spread into a smooth winding, which restores a floor.
2. It makes a sharp prediction on your 2D slab. A smooth pair-block winding costs `16 pi kappa k^2 b0^2 ln(L/xi)` per unit length on top of the quartic core — quadratic in k. That lifts the linear-in-k degeneracy and orders the partitions by the sum of k^2 over the rays: {1,1,1,1} (1.0) < {2,1,1} (1.5) < {2,2} (2.0) < {3,1} (2.5) < {4} (4.0). Your kappa = 0 order is different, so this is a falsifiable test of the term.
3. For a charge, ungauged kappa is excluded by the global-monopole linear divergence (M5 §11), but the gauge-covariant `kappa tr(DM DM)` is not: in the gauged radial reduction the energy stays finite for every kappa. That is the same term, with the same connection, that supplies the two missing Maxwell equations, so the repair of the strand and the repair of electromagnetism may be one term.

One constructive possibility to keep on record. If the wall tension goes to zero in the continuum — T_wall at h, h/2 and h/4, each relaxed, would say — then the quartic action puts no free-charge bound on delta at all. In that case delta = 0.2307, where your reader gives the vacuum the Koide biaxiality 0.382, is no longer excluded by free charge.

## The moment channel: one number worth a second look
Agreed that {3,1} is not a tail. But {1,1,1,1} reads 0.013 with slope -2.0, which is the falloff the moment criterion asks for. Before reading anything into it: a gap-zero line piercing a sphere removes a footprint of fixed area, a fraction ∝ r^-2 of the sphere, so a z-asymmetric pair of lines gives an l = 1 coefficient ∝ r^-2 by geometry alone. Masking a tube around each line and re-reading separates the two. If slope -2 survives outside the tubes with m = 0 content, it is the first field-derived moment candidate on a charged configuration; if it vanishes, it was footprint.

## The spin gate: why the physical generator cannot cancel here
On a relaxed charge in a biaxial vacuum the forced lines are off-axis and run to the boundary, so a physical rotation drags them round and their inertia scales with their length. That fits the halo fraction (0.5 to 0.7) and L96/L48 ≈ 2.04. Together with 017 it gives a dichotomy: for delta > 0 the strands make rotational inertia extensive, and for delta = 0 physical rotation is a symmetry with no collective coordinate. Either way, rigid rotation is not a normalisable spin. The explanation predicts C_rigid falling with the strand tension at smaller delta; your delta arm is non-monotone but capped, so it will test this once converged.
