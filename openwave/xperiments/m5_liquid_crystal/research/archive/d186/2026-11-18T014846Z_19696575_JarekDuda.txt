The growth slows in both sectors. Our local exponents at matched spacing are 0.91, 0.85, 0.81 and 0.67 (6→9, 9→12, 12→18, 18→27), beside your 0.927, 0.874 and 0.765. We agree that these boxes pick no law. Our reading, not a result: a size of its own of roughly 30–45 at L₁ = 0.3 is likely.
2. δ = 0.05 with L₁ = 0.3 is one curve in both codes. Your 3.83 and 6.58 on half-boxes 6 and 12, and our 3.82 (either stencil) and 5.15 on half-boxes 6 and 9, give shares of 0.64, 0.57 and 0.55. Your 370.2 with 28.1 inside r < 2 is our 370.8 with 28.1.
3. δ = 0.05 without L₁: the inertia is set by the grid. This is the spacing question your section 5 leaves open.
On half-box 6 at J = 0, with the mirror-averaged stencil, I = 1233, 2120 and about 3460 at spacings 0.5, 0.375 and 0.25. The last run is not converged and still rising. Your 2096 (521 inside r < 2) at 0.375 is our 2120 (532).
94–95 % of that inertia sits in walls one or two cells wide along the edges of the tilted lobes (panels c and e).
In the walls the gradients nearly commute: Σ‖[∂ₐM, ∂_bM]‖² / (Σ‖∂ₐM‖²)² is 0.008, 0.003 and 0.0007 at the three spacings, against 0.15 elsewhere.
∫‖∇M‖² times the spacing is 452, 481 and 464: the walls keep their amplitude and thin with the grid.
This is the flat direction at work at J = 0. The commutator quartic charges commuting gradients nothing, so only the grid sets the walls' width. The rigid-twist inertia does see them.
With L₁ = 0.03, 0.1 and 0.3 there are no walls, and the two spacings agree: I = 119.0 and 119.2, 182.0 and 182.2, 370.4 and 370.8 at 0.5 and 0.375. Each state has a flat loop, of radius 3.13, 3.5 and 3.82.
At L₁ = 0.01 (spacing 0.5 only) both kinds of state exist. Started from the radial hedgehog, the walls stay (U = 20.25, I = 309, no loop). Started from the L₁ = 0.03 ring, the state is a smooth ring (U = 18.49, I = 111, R = 3.04), and it is the lower one.
A prediction for a read you list as not computed (written before any such number from you): δ = 0.05, L₁ = 0, half-box 6, J = 0 at spacing 0.25 gives I above 3000, against your 2096 at 0.375, with the energy within 3 %.
None of this rescues L₁ as it stands: it costs the hedgehog 16πL₁ per unit of box and Coulomb beyond √(κ/L₁). What bounds commuting gradients without that cost is open.
