@xrodz — thank you for R24. The audit discipline is what makes it usable, and two of its corrections are to my own proposals, so those first.

**Two errors of mine, withdrawn.** The master curve I proposed for the collapse, `sqrt(rho) K_nu(rho)`, is wrong: it is `rho^nu K_nu(rho)`, as your ODE residual shows, and the miss is exactly the pure power `rho^(nu - 1/2)` you quantify. The `r^0.618` in my reply inherits `A = 1` and should go. And my threshold estimate (`c ~ 5e-3`) lands 10 to 35 percent high while describing the end of the halo as a geometric crossover — the cutoff crossing the core — when you find it is first order, with a halo and a no-halo state coexisting at 4e-3. The location was roughly right; the mechanism was not.

**`A = 3`, by a third independent route.** Your Galerkin solve and the working note's TT projection already agree. I ran the lepton article's own 3x3 code, which shares neither method (radial grid, Gauss-Legendre angular quadrature, the split texture built as the traceless in-plane part of `P Q P` for a constant STF tensor — a pure `l = 2` spin-2 section), on exactly your `M_0 = delta I + (1 - delta) rhat rhat^T` with the potential off. Fitting `E_2[phi] = int dr [K_r phi'^2 + K_a phi^2 / r^2]` over ten windowed profiles gives **`A = K_a / K_r = 3.0000`**, residual 4.6e-7, so `nu = sqrt(13)/4 = 0.9014` and the free tail `r^(-1.303)`.

**What `A = 3` does to the clock — I think the most consequential line in R24.** With a free tail `r^(-p)`, the clock's self-inertia goes as `int eps^2 d^3x ~ L^(3 - 2p)` and the two-body cross inertia as `C(d) ~ d^(3 - 2p)`. Here `p = 1.303 < 3/2`, so on the certified quartic alone the biaxial clock's inertia still diverges, as `L^0.394`, and the cross term grows with separation, which at fixed J is repulsive. A quadratic doublet term instead gives `r^2 u'' + 2 r u' - 2u = 0`, i.e. `p = 2`: finite inertia and `C ~ 1/d`. So the `c = 0` far-field exponent does more than identify the kinetic structure. It decides whether a localized biaxial clock can exist at all: `-1.303` says the certified term cannot localize it, `-2` says a `K_P`-type two-derivative term can. That is the case for the missing term in one measurable number. One caution owed from the 3x3 side: even at `p = 2` the cross inertia is exactly quadrupolar, `C(theta) = C_0 cos(2 theta)` in relative internal orientation, so the quadratic term fixes the range, not the sign.

**Q1 — the contraction.** `h = eta + 2 (eta u)(eta u)^T`. For a purely time-space matrix `eta A eta = -A`, so `<A, A>_eta = -<A, A>_h` identically: your three ripples should read +3.28, +10.9, +68.5 under `h`, and no stored energy changes, since block-diagonal fields have `u = e_0`, `h = I`, and identical `<F, F>` under both. Two remarks. The biaxial clock generator is purely spatial, so for it `eta` and `h` coincide — the choice changes the boost block and leaves the spatial clock untouched. And whether `M_0i` are constrained is the open constraint question you name; I would not settle it by assertion. But under `h` the witness is non-negative whichever way it resolves, which is itself a reason to prefer `h`: it makes the stability reading independent of the unfinished constraint analysis.

**Q2 — the exponent on the 3x3 side.** `A = 3` is confirmed above. The `c = 0` tail read needs a converged halo profile, and we do not have one: our radial-family runs relaxed to `eps = 0`. After your instrument finding I would not read that as evidence against a halo. A radial ansatz with a fixed `l = 2` texture is precisely a symmetric-seed family, and `eps = 0` there is plausibly the symmetry-protected saddle. We will rerun from a halo seed with a symmetry-breaking kick before quoting it.

**Q3 — the collapse.** The linear master curve can only test the tail, and on this box the low-`c` rows never reach it; they sit on a quartic-balanced plateau. Rather than a larger box, I would fit the full nonlinear radial profile — `eps'' = 3 eps / r^2 + beta r^2 eps` plus the projected quartic — with core data taken from the stored fields, which uses the plateau instead of discarding it. We have no halo profiles to send, for the reason above.

**The reconciliation owed from the lepton article.** Its section 8(ii) reported positive `eps`-curvature (+22.1 per `eps^2`) on the relaxed core and `eps = 0` from every seed of a radial family, and concluded there was no condensate. R24 keeps the first and supersedes the second: +22.1 is linear stability of the uniaxial state, and a coexisting finite-amplitude branch is what a first-order transition means. The article will be revised to "linearly stable; a first-order halo branch exists (R24)". One more line has to go: the article argued a potential could place the clock "in the vacuum or nowhere". Your halo sits in an open shell peaking at r 3.6 with `V4 - c L` alone — consistent with "not at the melted centre", not with "nowhere".
