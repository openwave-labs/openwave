Capstone v3.96 is on Zenodo (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23994958, all versions; every script is in the source zip). Before T11 writes the M5 rows, here are three results that bear on them.

1. **EM waves.**
   - For one frame field, every component of eq. 42's F = [∂M, ∂M] is the pullback of a 2-form from SO(3). So E·B ≡ 0 identically, static or moving, at all orders (p. 89, `v396/tetrad16/EdotB_check.py`). Crossed beams and an electron's charge plus dipole both have E·B ≠ 0.
   - Two exact null waves of eq. 42 do not superpose: the crossed field misses the equations by 20–40 % (p. 81, `crossing_null_waves.py`).
   - So linear light is not native to a single frame. It needs an imported A; in a uniaxial exterior the frame's orientation is gauge and TP4 reduces to R1 (p. 13). I'd read the EM-waves row as imported, not partial.
2. **Clock.** The founding quartic is a sum of Gram determinants of the frame rates (p. 93). A turning clock, rigid or radiating, therefore lowers and never raises the hedgehog's tilt stiffness: it is centrifugal, not stabilizing. Nothing changes in (a) at fixed J.
3. **Core.**
   - With the minor gap free and no L₁, the melted spherical core is a saddle above a weakly biaxial ring.
   - The ring's energy is not converged in angular resolution, and an L₁-type gradient term that adds 24 % to the core energy removes the ring (pp. 97–98).
   - So in (a), the runs without L₁ and the minor-gap map matter. The spinor sign there is at best energetic, since the spatial block's target space is linear.

We now apply this gate to every relaxation (p. 99), after three of our numbers turned out to be artefacts along rank-one gradients that the quartic does not see. It may be useful for your certification too:
- a manufactured flat-family field must reach its derived h² coefficient;
- a mirror test at round-off;
- two discretizations agreeing under refinement;
- cell-integrated against nodal potential;
- the lowest physical Hessian mode, with zero modes projected out.

The main post, with M5.41 and (a), stays for after (a).
