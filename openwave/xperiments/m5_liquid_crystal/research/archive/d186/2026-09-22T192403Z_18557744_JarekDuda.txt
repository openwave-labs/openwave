<h2>Answers to the four questions</h2>
<p><strong>1. What one charge drags.</strong> Topology fixes only the total: every enclosing sphere of a unit charge carries transverse
index 2 (the Euler number of n*TS² is deg(n)·χ = 2Q). How that total is split is not fixed by topology, and at the
Bogomolny point it does not matter:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>The straight half-disclination in your block sector (only the (2,3) pair moves) is a <strong>Bogomolny line</strong>. With the
transverse block s₀𝟙 + b(cos φ σ_z + sin φ σ_x), the gradient energy is u = 32(bb′/ρ)² and the potential is
W·K·(b² − b₀²)², with K = 4 + 36s₀² + 144s₀⁴. Then</p>
<p><strong>T = π√(32WK)·b₀⁴ = (π/√2)·Δ⁴√W</strong> at s₀ = 0, attained by b² = b₀²(1 − e^{−κρ²}) with κ = √(WK/32).</p>
<p>Numerics agree with the bound to 10⁻⁶. This is exactly your identity E_u ∝ δ⁴, and it predicts <strong>T ∝ √w with core width
∝ w^{−1/4}</strong>, i.e. your STRAND_W_LAW with the constant given.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>At this point the <strong>tension is additive in the index</strong>. One index-2 half-line, two index-1 lines, or four half-disclinations
all cost the same per unit length. So n_half is for the dynamics (beyond the block sector) to choose, and I claim no
preferred split. Please read it from the field as you planned.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>The ± slope does not depend on the split.</strong> The segment between an opposite pair carries index 2 in every arrangement:
each core sphere has index 2 and the far sphere 0. So <strong>σ = 4·T_½</strong>, where T_½ is your slab strand (a half-turn). Your
T/2-versus-T distinction arises only if outer strands run to the wall instead of along the segment, which costs more length.</p>
</li>
<li>

on it.</p>

<p><strong>2. The W and the script.</strong> W is the coefficient in E = ∫[u + W·Σ_{p≤4}(tr M^p − C_p)²], with

run outward, and none is required on the segment. In a neutral medium they end on opposite charges. The like-pair energy
should be Coulomb plus d-independent outer-strand energy. The paper's §13 construction attains Coulomb exactly for an equal
like pair.</p>

<h2>One result for Packet C and the moment calculation you deferred</h2>
<p>The E·B ≡ 0 theorem holds for the 3×3 field <strong>at fixed eigenvalues</strong>. Where eigenvalues vary, their gradients supply a
fourth one-form. I tested a hedgehog with a through-vortex along the spin axis, a clock, and a melted vortex core
(<code>eb_outgoing.py</code>, <code>eb_delta_scaling.py</code>):</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>E·B vanishes in two cases:</strong> at fixed eigenvalues (10⁻⁹), and exactly for a static twist along the vortex. It becomes
0.05–0.24 of |F|² with a melted core and the clock.</li>
<li><strong>The clock's sense decides the multipole.</strong> A rigid rotation about the spin axis gives dipole parity (B_r odd, zero net
flux). A uniform sense about the local director gives net magnetic flux, i.e. a magnetic charge.</li>
<li><strong>But the field is too weak outside a strongly biaxial region.</strong> It lives only where eigenvalues vary, and |B|/|E| ∝ δ²:
7×10⁻², 5×10⁻³, 3×10⁻⁴, 2×10⁻⁵ for δ = 0.29 → 0.0045. The electron needs |B|/|E| ≈ λ̄/2r, about ½ at its Compton
radius, so a weakly biaxial exterior falls short by six orders.</li>
</ul>
<p>So the exterior moment still needs the boost sector (or a separate connection), which is where your time–space constraint
analysis sits. That makes it the gate for Packets B and C.</p>
