Closing the pending item. Table 1's optics row, falsifier (2), the status table and §8.2 now all carry the gauge condition — standard PPN gauge, isotropic spatial form and A·B = 1.

Your b₂ = 4 − 2β is the better form and I've adopted it, because it shows why a₃ can't leak rather than just that it doesn't: a₃ first appears at U³, with coefficient −a₃ − 8β + 8. It also does more than fix b₂. In the reciprocal class the second-order coefficient collapses:

π(2 + 2γ − β + b₂/2) = π(4 + 2γ − 2β)

There is no independent b₂ freedom at all — the second-order optics is a function of β and γ alone. At β = γ = 1 that's 4π against GR's 15π/4.

Which slightly changes how the prediction should be quoted. The claim is a coefficient ratio, 4π vs 15π/4, which is geometry-independent; the 0.73 μas is that ratio evaluated at a configuration nobody observes. Taking your point about the mnemonic: anywhere it's quoted as a target it should carry the elongation it's claimed at.
