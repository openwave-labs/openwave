## 2. The hold: what fixed fields show

We write $x$ for the separation axis, $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \lambda_3$ for the eigenvalues of $M$, $g_{12} = \lambda_1 - \lambda_2$, and take the degree density $\rho$ as your charge density, the one ported in M5.54: the divergence of the pulled-back area form of the lifted long axis. A half is the set of nodes with $x > 0$ or $x \lt 0$.

$$
Q_\pm = \frac{h^3}{4\pi} \sum_{\pm} \rho , \qquad D = \frac{h^3}{4\pi} \Big( \sum_{+} \lvert x \rvert \rho + \sum_{-} \lvert x \rvert \rho \Big)
$$

**The lift.** With the lift radial about each ring's center, the half that holds the second ring reads $Q_- = -0.500$ on the relaxed pair, with 1228 sign flips of the lift on its boundary: around that ring the field is the half-turn image of a hedgehog. With the lift taken from the long axis of the pair seed (the product of two stereographic maps, centers at the rings), both halves read 0.9992 with no flip, and $D = 25.48$ against a reader's separation of 25.53. So the density does integrate to 1 in each half, with that lift.

**The staircase.** $\rho$ lives on the ring line, where $g_{12}$ closes. Moving the relaxed pair by one cell along $x$ on a straight path of fields, $D_+$ changes by 0.199 inside a displacement of 0.025, where the line crosses a row of nodes, and its gradient (taken with the lift fixed) misses the steps of the path by up to 0.119. A descent under a multiplier on $D$ would meet a staircase.

**What the plan holds in its place.** A weight of the line that is bounded by 1 at every node and normalized to 1 in each half:

$$
w = \exp\big( -g_{12}^2 / g_0^2 \big) , \qquad D'_\pm = \frac{\sum_{\pm} \lvert x \rvert w}{\sum_{\pm} w} , \qquad D' = D'_+ + D'_- , \qquad E_\mu = U + \frac{\mu}{2} \big( D' - d \big)^2
$$

with $g_0 = 0.2$. $g_{12}^2$ is a smooth function of $M$ where $\lambda_2 > \lambda_3$, and on the same path $D'$ follows its gradient within $8.3 \times 10^{-6}$ at every step. On the relaxed pair $D' = 25.534$ against 25.525 from the reader. The multiplier is applied as this quadratic penalty: at rest $f = \mu (d - D')$ is the multiplier, the slope of $U$ along the branch, and the hold energy never enters $U$. $D$ is read at every rung end beside $D'$.

**Against the failure of M5.50.** One far node with its gap closed moves $D'_+$ by 0.102 at a fixed-field cost of 1.67 in $U$, and drops the share of the weight that lies on the line from 0.999999 to 0.988; a rung whose share falls under 0.99 gives no point. Before any ladder, one control holds a single relaxed ring at a center moved by 1 and by 2: the fitted center must follow, the radius must stay that of the ring, the energy may rise by no more than a rigidly moved copy costs, the weight must stay on the line, and the released ring must return.

**The merged end.** The merged ring reads $D' = 8.44$ ($D = 7.69$, outside the regime of that read, since the line crosses the plane that splits the halves). A held separation therefore does not place the merged object at the small end of a ladder that starts from the pair.

## 3. The three rungs

**A. The free pair in larger boxes (your (a)).** Half-boxes 24 and 30 at spacing 0.5 ($97^3$ and $121^3$), with half-box 18 as the point on record: three boxes, since one larger box alone cannot tell a separation that stops from one that follows the box. Two starts per box, one at the separation on record (25.5) and one with each ring 5.24 from its face (37.5 and 49.5), so that an end is start-independent before it gets a word. The faces hold the far field of two hedgehogs 14 apart, as on record; one control on half-box 24 holds the degree-2 map about the box center instead. Words: KEEPS SEPARATING (the separation grows by more than 1.0 at each box step, and the second step is at least half the first; FOLLOWS THE BOX is printed beside it when the mean distance of the rings to their faces stays within 0.5 of 5.24), STOPS (the two larger boxes agree within 1.0), MERGES (both larger boxes end as one object), or no label. A box counts only when its two starts end alike, and the control can withhold the word: if it ends more than 1.0 away from the other starts of its box, the word reads no label, the far field moving the separation.

**B. The energy against a held separation (your (b)).** Half-box 18 at spacings 0.5 and 0.375. Two ladders of targets of $D'$: inward from the free pair (24 down to 2, fourteen rungs, each from the rung above), and outward from the merged object (10 up to 24, six rungs). At 0.375 each rung starts from its 0.5 parent, interpolated. Read at every rung: $U$, $f$, $D'$, $D$, the reader's separation, and whether the state is two rings or one object. Where both ladders hold a state at one level with different $U$, both are printed as branches. A second hold strength ($\mu = 1$ beside $\mu = 20$) at three targets checks that the curve does not depend on it.

**The number.** Yours, from your post of 2026-11-24: the maximum along $d$ of the energy above $E_{\min}$, in units of the core energy. The plan reads it over the held levels with the merged end included, in units of half the free pair's $U$ on the same grid (questions 4 and 7):

$$
M_c = \frac{\max\big( \max_d E_{\mathrm{low}}(d) , \ U_{\mathrm{merged}} \big) - E_{\min}}{E_{\mathrm{ring}}}
$$

with $E_{\mathrm{low}}(d)$ the least energy found on the level $d$ by either ladder. It is read as found: a lower state on some level that neither ladder reaches would lower it. A profile that only rises to the merged object gives $M_c = 0.0207$ at spacing 0.5 from the record alone. Whether the largest level lies between the ends (an interior maximum) is printed beside it.

**C. One ring turned at held separations (your (c)).** At up to three separations among the inward rungs where the collar fits (targets 13 to 16), at spacing 0.5: the table of $U$ along the turn from $2\pi$ to 0 on fixed fields, about the ring's normal and an in-plane axis, and one descent of the fully turned start under the same hold. A node-wise turn keeps every eigenvalue, so the turned start meets the hold exactly. With free cores the turn has a falling path (M5.56), so a turn that stays would be tabled as a stalled descent or a state the path does not reach, not as protection. The collar needs room between the ring's own tube, the other ring and the faces, which leaves separations of about 12.9 to 17.1: this rung cannot say what the turned class costs at contact.
